Attention is a
place to return to.

A note on focus, friction, and choosing what gets to stay.

The day rarely arrives as a blank page. It comes already crowded: open tabs, half-finished thoughts, small requests waiting for an answer. Attention is not the absence of this noise. It is the practice of returning.

We tend to treat focus as a personal resource, something we either possess or have misplaced. But attention is also an environment. A clear desk, a single document, a phone in another room: these are modest acts of architecture. They make the next good choice easier.

“The quality of our attention is the beginning of the quality of our experience.”

The useful question is not how to become endlessly productive. It is what deserves a little more of the mind, and what can be allowed to remain unfinished. A life with edges is easier to inhabit than a life that keeps expanding.

So I am trying a smaller rhythm: read closely, write one honest paragraph, finish one thing before opening another. The practice is ordinary. That may be why it works.
